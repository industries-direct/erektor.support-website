## Safety Information

### ⚠️ Critical Safety Warnings

**BEFORE starting any leg maintenance:**

1. **Power down the equipment completely**
2. **Engage all safety locks** if applicable
3. **Relieve hydraulic pressure** using the pressure relief valve (refer to equipment manual)
4. **Never work under raised equipment** without proper support
5. **Keep all personnel clear** of the work area
6. **Do not attempt major repairs alone** - have qualified assistance

### Required Safety Equipment

- Protective gloves (heavy-duty leather)
- Safety glasses
- Steel-toed boots
- First aid kit nearby
- Fire extinguisher accessible
- Proper lifting equipment for leg assemblies

### Pressure Relief Procedure

1. Locate the **pressure relief valve** (red handle on main cylinder)
2. **Slowly turn counterclockwise** until no resistance is felt
3. **Verify pressure gauge reads 0** on the controller display
4. **Wait 30 seconds** before proceeding with maintenance

Each leg assembly consists of:

```

├── Hydraulic Cylinder
│   ├── Piston Rod
│   ├── Cylinder Housing
│   └── End Caps

│   ├── Position Transducer

│   ├── Pressure Line (In)
```

## Routine Maintenance

### Daily Visual Inspection (No Tools Required)

Perform this inspection before each day's operations:

1. **Visually inspect each leg** for:
   - Visible cracks or damage
   - Hydraulic fluid leaks
   - Bent or misaligned components
   - Loose connectors or bolts

2. **Check all hydraulic lines** for:
   - Leaks or weeping
   - Cracks or abrasions
   - Proper routing without kinks

3. **Verify positioning sensors** are intact:
   - Check electrical connectors
   - Ensure sensor is properly mounted
   - No broken wires exposed

4. **Log any findings** in your maintenance record

### Weekly Deep Inspection

**Tools needed:** Wrench set, hydraulic fluid test kit

1. **Check all connection points**:
   - Upper ball joint - should rotate freely
   - Lower ball joint - should rotate freely
   - Attachment bolts - should be snug but not over-tight
   - Use a wrench to verify tightness (torque spec: 45-55 ft-lbs)

2. **Inspect hydraulic connections**:
   - **Quick-disconnect couplers**: Press in/out should be smooth, no leaks when disconnected
   - **Thread connections**: No weeping or dripping between operations
   - **Hose condition**: No swelling, cracks, or thin spots

3. **Test sensor function**:
   - **Via controller**: Navigate to Diagnostics → Leg Sensors
   - **Verify reading** changes smoothly as equipment moves
   - **Check for gaps** - position sensor should follow actual position

4. **Hydraulic fluid condition**:
   - Use test kit to check for: water content, particle contamination
   - If contaminated, schedule fluid change
   - Fluid should be: clear reddish/amber color, no sludge

5. **Record all findings** with dates and any remedial actions taken

### Monthly Maintenance

**Time Required:** 1-2 hours per leg | **Tools needed:** Complete tool set, replacement seals kit

1. **Perform all weekly inspections** first

2. **Ball joint lubrication**:
   - Locate the grease fittings on upper and lower joints
   - Use a grease gun to inject 3-4 pumps of high-temperature grease
   - Work the joint back and forth to distribute
   - Wipe away excess grease

3. **Cylinder rod inspection**:
   - Visually inspect for scoring or corrosion
   - Light surface rust is normal; deep pitting requires replacement
   - Clean rod with clean cloth and light oil

4. **Seal condition check**:
   - Excessive weeping requires seal replacement
   - This is a job for trained technicians - contact support

5. **Bolt torque verification**:
   - Re-check all structural bolts
   - Torque values: 45-55 ft-lbs for standard connections
   - Adjust as needed if loose

## Diagnostics & Troubleshooting

### Leg-Specific Issues

#### Leg Not Moving or Responding Slowly

**Possible causes:**
- Hydraulic fluid low
- Clogged hydraulic filter
- Positioning sensor malfunction
- Damaged hydraulic line
- Piston rod sticking

**Troubleshooting:**
1. Check fluid level in main reservoir (should be at "Full" line when equipment is lowered)
2. Run controller diagnostics: Settings → Diagnostics → Leg Test
3. Move each leg individually and note response time
4. If only one leg affected, that leg likely needs maintenance/replacement
5. If all legs slow, may be system-wide (fluid, filter, pump)

#### Leg Drift or Position Inaccuracy

**Possible causes:**
- Calibration needed
- Sensor malfunction
- Hydraulic seal degradation
- Load imbalance

**Troubleshooting:**
1. Recalibrate system: Settings → Maintenance → Calibration
2. If drift persists, check sensor readings in diagnostics
3. Verify equipment is on level ground for calibration
4. Schedule maintenance if recurring

#### Audible Noises from Leg

**Possible causes:**
- Hydraulic cavitation
- Worn bearing
- Loose bolts
- Contaminated hydraulic fluid

**Solutions:**
- **Clicking/popping**: May indicate cavitation; check fluid level and filter
- **Grinding**: Likely bearing wear; schedule leg replacement
- **Rattling**: Loose bolts; tighten all connections
- **Hissing**: Air in system; bleed air from cylinders

#### Visible Leaks

**Action based on leak location:**

| Location | Severity | Action |
|----------|----------|--------|
| Around piston rod (at seal) | High | Stop operations; schedule maintenance dispatch |
| Quick-disconnect coupler | Medium | Check coupler seals; may need cleaning or replacement |
| Hydraulic line (pinhole leak) | High | Immediate dispatch - safety hazard |
| Joint connector drip | Low | Clean and monitor; schedule inspection |

## Field Leg Replacement

### When to Call for Dispatch

[Request immediate dispatch](../request-dispatch.html) if:
- **Leg completely non-functional** (won't move)
- **Visible structural damage** (cracks, bends)
- **Major hydraulic leak** (spurting or significant weeping)
- **Equipment cannot achieve level position**
- **Safety is compromised**

### Professional Replacement Process

Our technicians follow this standard procedure:

1. **Safety Setup**
   - Clear the work area
   - Power down equipment completely
   - Engage all safety locks

2. **Pressure Relief**
   - Locate and operate pressure relief valve
   - Confirm zero pressure on gauge

3. **Leg Disconnection**
   - Disconnect hydraulic quick-couplers (press tabs)
   - Disconnect electrical sensor connector
   - Remove attachment bolts (typically 4-6 bolts)
   - Carefully lower and remove old leg assembly

4. **New Leg Installation**
   - Position replacement leg assembly
   - Install attachment bolts (torque to spec: 45-55 ft-lbs)
   - Connect electrical sensor connector
   - Connect hydraulic quick-couplers (should snap firmly)

5. **Testing and Verification**
   - Slowly raise pressure and test for leaks
   - Move new leg through full range of motion
   - Verify positioning sensor readings
   - Confirm equipment is level

6. **Calibration**
   - Run system calibration via controller
   - Perform full equipment test
   - Document work in maintenance record

**Typical replacement time: 1-2 hours per leg**

### DIY Replacement (Advanced Users Only)

**This is NOT recommended unless you are certified and trained.** However, if you choose to perform replacement yourself:

#### Materials Needed

- Replacement leg assembly (correct model and specification)
- Hydraulic fluid (if lines are broken)
- New seals and gaskets (replacement kit)
- Complete tool set
- Safety equipment

#### Step-by-Step Process

1. **Review this entire guide** - especially safety section

2. **Pressure relief** (see safety section above)

3. **Support the equipment**:
   - Use heavy lifting equipment or blocks
   - Never rely solely on the hydraulic system
   - Ensure safety support is rated 2x the leg load

4. **Disconnect old leg**:
   - Remove bolts systematically (don't drop leg)
   - Capture hydraulic fluid if lines will be open
   - Label all connections

5. **Install new leg**:
   - Position carefully
   - Bolt installation sequence: Cross-pattern (like tire lug bolts)
   - Install and snug first pass, then torque to spec
   - Connect hydraulics carefully to avoid spills

6. **Air bleed** (if hydraulics were opened):
   - Carefully apply pressure
   - Watch for air bubbles in fluid
   - May require multiple pressure cycles
   - Consult equipment manual for air bleed procedure

7. **Calibration**:
   - System calibration is CRITICAL after replacement
   - Follow controller calibration procedure
   - Test thoroughly before resuming operations

## When to Replace

### Signs a Leg Needs Replacement

A leg should be replaced when:

1. **Won't move or respond** to controller commands
2. **Visible structural damage**: cracks, bends, or breaks
3. **Major hydraulic leak** that cannot be sealed
4. **Piston rod is scored, pitted, or deeply corroded**
5. **Position sensor is broken or unresponsive**
6. **More than 3 seal leaks** in the same leg
7. **Exceeded service life**: Typically 5-10 years depending on usage
8. **Performance degradation** cannot be resolved through maintenance

### Leg Service Life

Standard leg assemblies are designed for:
- **Service Life**: 5-10 years typical
- **Cycle Rating**: 100,000-500,000 cycles depending on model
- **Pressure Rating**: Varies by model (typically 2,000-3,000 PSI)

Factors affecting service life:
- Operating conditions (frequent heavy use reduces life)
- Maintenance quality (well-maintained legs last longer)
- Environmental conditions (extreme heat/cold reduces life)
- Hydraulic fluid quality (contaminated fluid causes premature wear)

## Common Questions

**Q: How often should I replace the legs?**
A: Typically every 5-10 years, depending on usage. Professional inspection can determine remaining service life.

**Q: Can I mix old and new legs?**
A: Yes, you can. However, all legs should be the same model/specification. Mixing different generations may reduce accuracy.

**Q: What if a leg fails during operations?**
A: [Request immediate dispatch](../request-dispatch.html). Equipment can often still function on three legs at reduced capacity, but immediate repair is recommended for safety.

**Q: How much does a leg replacement cost?**
A: Costs vary by model ($2,000-$8,000 for parts). Contact support for a quote.

**Q: Can I perform maintenance myself?**
A: Routine maintenance (inspection, lubrication) can be done by trained operators. Complex repairs and replacements should be done by certified technicians.
